WHEN THE SKY CLEARS — ART GAME STORY AND UI DIRECTION
20 October 2026

Make the player learn how this child waits for them. Then make waiting change
from a game, to a danger, to a kindness. At the end, let him decide when to go.

The emotional promise is: I cannot put the world back. I can stay with you.
The dread is: the things I know how to do may stop being enough.
The heartbreak is: the person I am protecting is learning to expect loss.
The release is small: for once, he reaches first, and she follows.

This is a game adaptation of the established two-child journey. The Girl's
braid and faded blue ribbon, the smaller Boy, painted layers, vulnerable side
view, and the passage from ordinary warmth into ash remain the visual spine.
Their family relationship is unspecified. The film handoff's 100-second timing
and music selection do not dictate the playable game's length or soundtrack.
The five existing game chapters are a focused adaptation, not a retelling of
every episode. Owen, Helen, the farmhouse and the Boy's return to speech need
their own continuity-aware treatment if the game later expands to those events.

THE PLAYER'S EMOTIONAL ACTIONS

Walking together is the sentence. Separation, stopping, reaching and returning
are its punctuation. Build the most important story into these actions before
adding more exposition, puzzles or miles of road.

Repeat three ordinary actions with changed circumstances:

1. Wait. In safety, he is distracted by something worth looking at. In danger,
   asking him to wait means leaving him in a place the player must remember.
   Later, the player waits because he needs time, even when the road is clear.

2. Make room. Initially he squeezes beside her to look through a warm window.
   At a shelter, the roof is useful only when both children fit beneath it.
   At the school, he makes room for her. Care starts moving in both directions.

3. Take a hand. Begin with play, repeat with necessity, finish with permission.
   Contact needs readable negative space, weight and a reachable wrist. Let
   the player notice a missing hand before a message explains the separation.

Care is an action with visible consequences, never a goodness score. A repaired
roof stops a particular drip and lets him relax. Food changes how they sit and
how soon they can stand. A quiet pause earns no currency. The reason to do it
is that he is there. Optional moments must be safe enough to remain in.

THE FIVE CHAPTERS

01  BEFORE THE SILENCE — give the player something specific to miss.

Spend the opening on ordinary life before asking the player to conserve it.
Let the Boy get ahead, balance, turn and wait. Teach returning to one another
while it is still play. Establish one recurring spatial composition: her hand
held out, him on the other side of a small gap, a warm sound behind them.
The source's puddle is ideal: crossing it is initially unnecessary and fun.

The first unsettling change should be an absence the player can discover.
An unchanged window is now unlit. A washing line has no clothes. The familiar
room tone loses a voice before smoke becomes visible. Keep the route readable
and the controls dependable. Doubt belongs to the world, not to input response.

At the last warm hearth: “He holds out his hand.” / “There is still time to
walk slowly.” This ordinary gesture supplies the ending's remembered image.

02  WHERE THE BIRDS WENT — teach the player to listen for what is missing.

The early woods allow an expectation to form, then withhold its answer. A tree
that should hold birds stays quiet. Water still moves. Their two sets of feet
remain distinct. Distance ahead is visible, but its safety is uncertain.

Tie the canal choice to both bodies. Cold water is something the player has
to bring him through; the dry high route asks for shared work and a scarce
piece of salvage. Neither route carries a moral verdict. Show the consequence
in damp clothes, pace, breathing and the next refuge, with accurate UI support.

Give the player a quiet return after the crossing. Do not place a second scare
at every relief point. Dread requires some relief the player can believe in.

In the hollow: “She stops. He takes one more step before he notices.” /
“Then he comes back.” The player can leave when they choose.

03  UNDER A BORROWED ROOF — let shelter reveal what escape could not.

Make closing the door and stopping the leak physical, audible accomplishments.
Outside noise should become a room sound: drip, stove, cloth, settling timber.
The player finally has time to look at him. He keeps his coat on. She leaves
him that choice. Do not explain the coat, diagnose him, or turn this into a
question the player must answer correctly.

The core scene is a permission to stop. Let the player remain after the useful
rest action ends. Supplies and hazard clocks hold during the optional shared
pause. The next road can wait. This is where tenderness can become painful:
the player has made a room safe, but cannot make it home.

After departure, carry a small thing from this refuge forward in composition
or foley. Specific continuity will do more than another generic ruined block.

04  THE LAST SCHOOL — ordinary arrangements outlive the people who made them.

Let order do the work: coats at child height, two chairs, an unfinished lesson,
marks recording someone growing. Resist turning every object into a death
reveal. An unfinished tomorrow hurts because it was once routine.

Author a bounded piece of acting in which he straightens a chair and leaves
room. The player can wait or pass by; the game must acknowledge either without
blame. The current playable pass establishes the pause and text; chair acting
and a registered prop still need production. Keep that distinction visible.

Echo the earlier puddle in the flooded gallery. What used to be play now costs
warmth and time. Leave a recognisable composition or sound to connect them.
Do not require a paragraph explaining the parallel.

At the refuge: “He makes room beside him.” / “She stays until he is ready.”
The Girl's protection is starting to allow his choices, rather than replacing
them. He remains a child with preferences, not an escort objective.

05  THE HAND THAT STAYED — allow one choice that survival has not dictated.

Reduce spectacle. Leave air around the children. Let an ordinary living thing
occupy a small part of the ash rather than restoring the whole landscape.
Wind, ground, cloth and footsteps should remain legible without a victory cue.

Repeat the opening hand composition. At the final doorway, he steps ahead
using the actual character controller, stops, and offers contact back to her.
She waits. The reversal is small enough to believe. It does not repair their
home, restore his speech, or resolve every person they have lost.

If the player shared the opening pause, the final line repeats “He holds out
his hand.” Then: “This time, she follows.” If they missed it, use an observation
that stands alone: “He takes one step ahead.” / “She lets him.” No better-ending
rank, completion requirement or punishment attaches to either version.

Hold the world. Let the player remain. After they choose to continue, the
ending offers rest, credits and a return to title. Omit elapsed time, death
count and things-collected totals. The player has accompanied two children,
not submitted a performance for grading.

UI DIRECTION

The default playing frame leaves the painting and children dominant. Survival
meters recede when healthy, return before critical resource thresholds, and
stay available in the pack. Settings offer an always-visible alternative.
Warnings, separation cues, sound detection and held-action progress remain
readable when relevant. Dread does not justify concealing actionable danger.

Q should describe the action it will perform: WAIT HERE or COME WITH ME.
Use an explicit location cue when he falls behind or gets ahead. Keep this
language factual. Do not make a companion navigation failure look intentional
and then accuse the player of neglect.

One text focus at a time: chapter title, immediate warning, or a short notice.
The practical next task belongs in the pack; avoid printing a long objective
across the chapter entrance. A dark local backing supports small text against
both pale fog and charcoal. An alert must say what the player can do.

The pack carries finite supplies, condition meters, a practical next task and
remembered pages. Pages can be browsed to the end. Do not imply missing story
with empty numbered collection slots. Page navigation is reading navigation,
not a completion percentage. Keep interface language out of the child's voice.

Shared pauses retain the visible world. One line fades in after a breath;
another follows without a dialogue box or a spoken performance. There is no
automatic exit. E, Space, movement or the visible return button can leave.
Esc pauses and returns to the same moment. No input must be held indefinitely.

SOUND AND ACTING THAT STILL NEED AUTHORING

The current ambient loop cannot carry this whole arc. Compose distinct warm
ordinary-life, absent-life, exposed-route and sheltered-room layers. Establish
a sound in safety before removing it. Preserve the Boy's silence and source
continuity; do not add crying, humming, laughter or a voice without the story
decision that permits it. The trailer's selected recording is not a game bed.

Keep the children audible as two bodies: staggered steps, coat movement, a
different landing. Give cover a useful change of sound. Establish the sound
of an approaching threat early enough for a fair decision. More constant
rumble would erase contrast. The first pass only recedes the existing ambience
during pauses and later chapters; that is not a finished sound design.

Prioritise these authored performances: playful stop-and-wait; first reluctant
release of a hand; protective fold and delayed rise; settling under the repaired
roof; making room at the school; final offered hand and unhurried step. Keep
the entire pose readable at the actual playing camera. A moving limb does not
yet establish intent, trust, fear or weight.

IMPLEMENTATION AND NEXT REVIEW

Implemented in this first pass: five data-authored shared pauses at existing
refuges, real shelter/contact checks, optional held entry, a held survival
clock, gentle paired facing/contact, return control, remembered care pages,
save-compatible flags, the ending's native step, an opening-sensitive final
line, a quiet default HUD, a visibility preference, browsable memories, and
an ending without performance totals. Original character and painted assets
remain the source. This pass adds no new farmhouse event or spoken dialogue.

Next production work, in order: author a short warm playable opening; establish
and then remove one recognisable environmental sound; give the roof a specific
before/after performance; make the school chair gesture physical; review the
opening/final hand rhyme at normal playing scale. These need custom staging,
sound, drawing and human playtesting. A whole new chapter is lower priority.

Watch a person play without asking for an emotion. Notice whether they look
back without a prompt, stop before the Boy catches up, stay beyond the first
useful rest, and recognise the final offered hand. Ask what they expected to
happen, where they stopped feeling safe, and what they remember him doing.
If they remember only resource bars and gates, revise the performances and
rhythm. Native functional checks establish behavior; the player's response
establishes whether the art is working.
