1 PURPOSE AND CURRENT DECISIONS
Use this document to plan and implement the approved trailer in Blender, subject to the work Conrad assigns in the receiving Codex session. The finished hero characters must be DRAWN TWO DIMENSIONAL SILHOUETTES. Three dimensional characters or motion capture may be used as hidden movement references, never substituted for the final drawn children.
The project is When the Sky Clears, a war and displacement story about two children. It is separate from The Lions of Judah, The War on Leaves, DIRTY AIR and A Chair in the Rain. Do not import their characters or story beats.
Conrad approved the 100 second trailer described below and specified Grease Pencil, blocking, then painting. He subsequently clarified that a rigged animation should be matched to a 2D character beside it to get movement and gait right. The desired visual language combines Limbo-like silhouette readability and side-on staging with Arcane-like painterly color, lighting and atmosphere. Develop original designs rather than recreating either reference's characters or settings.
Confirmed direction
Exactly 1 minute 40 seconds, with an audio fade at the end.
One continuous-looking lateral journey. The children begin walking, skipping and playing; their movements become cautious, crouched, urgent and exhausted as the world changes.
The landscape changes behind and around them. Background explosions make them hunker down. The ground they navigate also changes.
Color gradually drains. The tone becomes quiet, haunting and dark.
WHEN THE SKY CLEARS appears as monumental block lettering in the landscape. The children move across it, letter to letter, with physical bases and edges affecting their route.
The final children are flat drawn silhouettes. Their gait should be informed by an animated reference and/or reference-driven 2D rig.
Approved trailer treatment choices
The supplied treatment uses no dialogue or narrator, a smooth music fade from 1:30 to 1:40, and a picture fade over the final two seconds. These are part of the approved treatment, not a rule for every future project. Do not return to the superseded 90 second dialogue montage or its separate piano/cello score.

3 APPROVED TRAILER PICTURE PLAN
0:00 to 0:18  BEFORE
The song begins over a warm painted landscape. The Girl and Boy walk left to right through long grass. He skips ahead, turns and walks backward to tease her. She catches up. They chase each other briefly, then leap over a shallow puddle. Their movement is loose and individual, not a synchronized marching loop.
Behind them are a bakery window, washing on a line, gardens and sunlight through trees. Their figures are dark, edged with gold; the Girl's blue ribbon catches light. The camera drifts alongside them.
0:18 to 0:35  SOMETHING CHANGES
They keep playing as the background changes before they notice. A passing tree hides a transition: the washing line is empty, shutters close, and a cart stands abandoned. The Boy hops along a low wall. The Girl follows with her arms out for balance. A thin smoke column rises behind them. Sunlight cools and greens lose their warmth. The music continues uninterrupted.
0:35 to 0:50  PLAY BECOMES CAUTION
The low wall becomes broken masonry. Their next playful jump lands among scattered bricks. The Boy begins another skip, then stops. A distant flash lights the clouds. The Girl looks back, raises her shoulders and reaches for his hand. They move faster and lower. The village transitions into tents and muddy ground. Warm windows become isolated fires.
0:50 to 1:07  THE WORLD BEHIND THEM
A distant explosion blooms behind the children. Its light precedes a low, muffled impact. Both hunker down; the Girl folds over the Boy protectively. They wait a breath, then run. Canvas, smoke and dark posts pass between the camera and the pair. These occlusions conceal the camp's transition into a ruined street. A fence becomes a barricade. The path drops into a crater. They scramble down and climb out together. Color drains toward blue-gray and charcoal, with traces of ember orange.
1:07 to 1:20  KEEP GOING
The camera follows their exhausted pace. Running becomes uneven. The Boy catches his foot. The Girl stops, turns and helps him up. Keep the small effort inside an enormous landscape rather than cutting to a conventional close-up. They pass a shattered school window. Torn paper moves along the ground. Another distant flash makes them duck without looking back. They now anticipate danger. They emerge into an almost empty stretch of ash.
1:20 to 1:30  THE TITLE BECOMES THE LANDSCAPE
Huge dark forms initially read as broken walls. As the camera tracks, they resolve into WHEN THE SKY CLEARS. The lettering occupies the landscape itself: weathered matte block forms against pale sky. The children pass in front of one letter and briefly behind a projecting edge of the next. Low broken bases extend into their path; they step over them and help each other through gaps. This remains survival, not bouncy platform-game movement.
1:30 to 1:36  SMALL AGAIN
Begin the smooth ten second music fade. Their run slows to a walk, still hand in hand. The camera gently widens without leaving the side-on axis so the complete title becomes readable. No victorious sunrise: only thin cold light separates the letters from sky. The children cross the final word, small beneath its scale.
1:36 to 1:40  QUIET
The camera stops. The children take a few more steps toward the right edge. Hold title and silhouettes together. Music and residual sound reach silence at 1:40. The image gently fades to black during the final two seconds. No final impact and no extra slogan.
Visual throughline: the puddle they once crossed for fun becomes a gap they must cross to survive. The hand that pulled the other into play becomes the hand that will not let go.
4 CHARACTER APPEARANCE AND DRAWING PACKAGE
Only two hero characters are needed for this trailer. Do not build Owen, Helen, Sarah, Tom or the full series cast for this first deliverable. Distant background people, if needed, can be simple original silhouette shapes.
The Girl: approximately ten or eleven in the source story, with a braid, a small faded-blue ribbon and a simple dress silhouette. The Boy is smaller, with untidy hair and a simple coat/trouser silhouette; exact age and proportions need design approval. Do not claim these are siblings unless the story establishes that relationship.
Create a shared scale sheet and side-profile design for both. Keep feet on a common baseline. Establish head size, shoulder height, hand height and reach. Make a small pose sheet containing neutral, walk contact, walk passing, run, crouch, reach and protective hunker. Check readability as solid black shapes before adding texture.
Suggested editable parts: head, torso/pelvis, near and far upper arms, forearms/hands, thighs, lower legs/feet. Use rigid bone parenting where appropriate and weighted deformation where necessary. Avoid excessive segmentation that makes joints look mechanical. The dress may need replacement silhouettes rather than being stretched like a trouser leg. Braid and ribbon need secondary motion. Keep hands simple but provide open, reaching, clasped and supporting drawings.
Use deep charcoal fills, selective edge light and restrained interior brushwork. Silhouette does not require a featureless black blob in every shot. Preserve negative space around hands and limbs, especially when the children touch. Do not add glowing eyes. Reference images establish mood, not final model approval.
5 MOTION REFERENCE AND TWO DIMENSIONAL RIG PLAN
Preferred experiment: an animated reference beside a drawn Grease Pencil puppet, both playing the same movement in strict side view. The reference can also be aligned underneath the drawing for pose comparison. Hide reference geometry and helper rigs from the final render, without disabling dependencies needed to drive the drawing.
Do not assume a Mixamo FBX can directly animate Grease Pencil without setup. Stock FBX animation drives a three dimensional armature; the drawing requires its own rig, weights or rigid parts, compatible coordinates, and deliberate motion mapping.
Proposed mapping experiment
1. Inspect the actual Blender version and supported Grease Pencil data/API. Work in a separate test file or a copy of an existing project.
2. Import one licensed walk reference. Preserve its source file and rig, record its frame rate, scale and forward axis, and inspect the actual motion.
3. Choose a fixed side camera and drawing plane. Match the child design's size to the reference while retaining child proportions.
4. Establish corresponding hips, shoulders, elbows, wrists, knees, ankles and feet. Use the reference's joint trajectories as a guide or project them into the drawing plane. Adjust segment lengths to the child, then solve the 2D pose instead of blindly copying all 3D bone rotations.
5. Constrain or key the silhouette's own controls. Keep offsets, depth ordering and near/far limbs explicit. If automated transfer is unstable, use the same reference for manual key poses; do not abandon the drawn character.
6. Bake a test only after the live mapping is understood. Keep an editable source copy and inspect the result at normal speed and frame by frame.
7. Fix foot contact, hips, knees, arm swing, balance and transitions. Adult mocap is not automatically a believable child gait.
8. Hide the reference for a clean silhouette-only render. Save, close, reopen and render again to verify persistence.
Root travel must match the planted foot and stride distance. An in-place cycle needs correctly paced scene travel; root-motion clips need deliberate extraction/retargeting. Do not apply both and double the travel. Retiming 30 fps reference onto a proposed 24 fps timeline must preserve the intended action duration, not merely change the frame-rate setting and slow everything down.
The reference supplies timing, footfall and weight-shift information. It does not decide the children's emotions or replace custom contact animation. For hand-holding, use a coordinated contact target or equivalent manual choreography, and adjust both bodies. Verify the pair can walk without wrists separating or arms stretching. Hunkering, helping up, wall balance and letter traversal need scene-specific poses and contact corrections.
6 MOTION CHECKLIST AND SOURCES
M01 Relaxed walk, slow walk and stop: opening and ending. Source candidate: Synty Walk Forward and Walk to Idle transitions; Rokoko walk/run pack. Adapt cadence and shared pauses.
M02 Skipping and playful steps: opening. Mixamo search suggestions: skipping, happy walk, playful. No exact clip verified. Custom asymmetry and gestures are likely.
M03 Playful and urgent running, then exhausted running: opening and middle. Synty Run Forward/Sprint F or Rokoko references. Fatigue requires custom timing and posture.
M04 Backward walk while teasing: opening. Synty Back Strafe B or Mixamo search walking backwards. Match gaze and footing.
M05 Puddle jump and landing: opening. Synty Jump Walking/Jump Running/Land Idle Soft. Match actual gap width, anticipation and landing.
M06 Low-wall balance: before danger. Mixamo searches balancing/balance walk/tightrope are unverified suggestions. Custom foot placements and counterbalance are essential.
M07 Hesitation and look back: first danger. Synty Head Look/Body Look can inform mechanics; custom held reaction and delayed next step carry meaning.
M08 Reach and take a hand: first danger and continued travel. Custom paired contact, grip and release. Generic reaching alone is insufficient.
M09 Crouch, duck and rise: repeated danger. Synty Stand to Crouch/Crouch to Stand; Mixamo crouching/ducking search suggestions. Match obstacle clearance.
M10 Protective fold over the Boy: camp blast. Custom paired pose. Mixamo cowering/cover head are reference search suggestions only.
M11 Crater descent and scramble out: camp/ruins. Synty slope motions can inform locomotion; hands, terrain contact, slipping and crest transitions require custom work.
M12 Stumble, help up and title-base navigation: late sequence. Mixamo stumbling/tripping/standing up/stepping over are unverified searches. Coordinate the children and the actual set geometry.
Initial reference selection: one walk, run, jump/land, crouch and stop transition. Prove the approach before collecting an exhaustive library.

Suggested depth bands

Gate 0  Inspect and plan

Gate 1  One child and one walk cycle
Agree the silhouette proportions. Place one animated side-view reference beside it. Build and test the 2D rig/motion mapping. Acceptance: planted feet, stable proportions, clear near/far limbs, no unwanted out-of-plane twisting, editable drawings and a successful save/reopen/render. This gate is proposed, not already completed.
Gate 2  Pair interaction
Add the second child. Test matched walking and hand contact, then a simple crouch. Acceptance: hands remain connected without rubber arms; both bodies respond; poses read in silhouette. Keep corrective keys and drawing substitutions editable.
Gate 3  Ten second emotional proof
Block a short transition from play to caution using plain silhouettes and gray set shapes. Acceptance: the change is understandable without dialogue or explanatory captions. This proof does not replace the full trailer; it validates the method before larger work.
Gate 4  Full 100 second rough animatic
Block the entire path, five zones, obstacles, title and camera. Place rough character actions and selected music excerpt. Review pacing and title readability before paint. Keep the two children within safe composition through all swaps and ramps. Preserve the approved story direction; request a decision if a major change is needed.
Gate 5  Grease Pencil performance pass
Finish cycles, action transitions, contact poses, hunkering, stumble/help-up and letter traversal. Add braid/dress/ribbon follow-through. Review motion in black silhouettes with simple backgrounds before surface detail.
Gate 6  Painting and atmosphere
Paint depth layers, retain matching geometry/contacts, add the gradual palette change and restrained effects. Check characters against the lightest and darkest backgrounds, not only a flattering still.
Gate 7  Edit and delivery
Finish sound, the final ten second audio fade and last two second image fade. Verify exactly 100 seconds at the confirmed rate, no leaked reference rigs, no missing external textures, no unintended silence/gaps, readable title and an editable project that reopens.
