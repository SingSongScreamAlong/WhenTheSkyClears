WHEN THE SKY CLEARS — ANIMATION AUDIT AND RECOVERY PLAN
19 October 2026 | Inspection and recommendations; no animation sources changed

The main shortfall is authored performance. The full-film branch still moves a
held drawing through the landscape. The separate walking branch can move limbs,
but does not yet make the children feel alert, weighted, individual or emotionally
connected. The newer first-danger shot repairs presentation and continuity while
retaining that limited performance. Repeated small costume and motion adjustments
have not solved the underlying acting problem.

The painted world, established designs and layered 2D/2.5D method are useful
foundations. Preserve them. The next production investment should be strong
full-body poses, timing, head/gaze drawings and believable paired interaction.

1. THE FILM DOES NOT YET CONTAIN THE DEVELOPED CHARACTER ANIMATION

Severity: critical for the film's animation quality.

Fresh v064 readback finds zero armatures in the world scene. Its visible actor is
"v016 | CHILDREN | held painterly near-age pair". That Grease Pencil object has
one layer, a single drawing at frame 1, no modifiers and location channels only.
The 100-second picture still has the held pair, even though color and world
movement advance. This is a coverage failure, independent of any walk's quality.

v053 is a separate ordinary-walk asset. v056 is an exploratory girl costume/body
study. v065f is a seven-second first-danger candidate with two character rigs.
v059 is an earlier-model recovery benchmark. Their version numbers do not mean
those improvements are assembled into one continuously acted film.

Delivery caveat: both full-cut MP4 paths advertised in v064's START_HERE are
absent at inspection time. RENDER_STATUS.json says its 2,400 world frames are
complete. Completed world frames and a native assembly do not establish the
advertised encoded full movies. The completed full movie verified here is v040.
Do not let an entry-point document become the evidence for animation coverage.

Action: establish one working animation assembly with an explicit component map.
After a performance segment passes review, integrate it and issue an updated
low-resolution full-cut review. Keep unanimated intervals visibly recorded as
unfinished in the production register.

2. PRIMARY BODY MECHANICS STILL READ AS A RESTRAINED PUPPET CYCLE

Severity: critical for the perceived quality of the newer character work.

The v053 chronology shows repeating strides beneath a largely stable upper-body
silhouette. Chest, head and pelvis relationships vary too little to create a
strong sense of accepting weight, passing over support and releasing into the
next step. The arms remain dominated by recurrent swing; the hands and face
contribute little change of intention. Both children share a closely related
rhythm and a fixed phase offset rather than independently motivated behavior.

Fresh measurements over 129 quarter-frame samples of one source cycle:
                     boy             girl
Pelvis angle span    3.20 degrees    2.40 degrees
Chest angle span     4.07 degrees    2.38 degrees
Head angle span      1.49 degrees    0.82 degrees
Pelvis height span   21.5 mm         21.5 mm

These are evaluated sagittal angles in rig coordinates. They are descriptive,
not pass/fail targets or a prescription to add arbitrary rotation and bounce.
The visual problem is the weak pose differentiation and repeated timing at the
actual viewing scale. Simply increasing every value could create worse motion.

The v053 builder deliberately preserves the old hip head, foot/boot paths and
travel, then compensates the legs around small pelvis/chest edits. That makes
sense for a controlled comparison, but it also limits how far the performance
can change. The next mechanics pass should permit newly designed support times,
pelvis motion, stride and foot paths when the action needs them. Preserve the
outcome of planted, coherent feet; do not make every old trajectory untouchable.
Any revised travel must be conformed with camera and terrain progression.

Action: draw/pose clearly distinct contact, compression, passing and release
relationships for both sides of one cycle. Check chest over pelvis, support-leg
loading, trailing heel release, swing-foot clearance and purposeful arm response.
Judge a rough silhouette at scene scale before rebuilding painted caches.

3. THE CHILDREN NEED A VISIBLE THOUGHT PROCESS

Severity: critical for the story.

In v065f the children walk and bring their hands together, but the head/profile,
gaze and upper-body action provide little readable transition from ordinary
walking to awareness of danger. The reach is visible; the decision motivating
it and the partner's response remain weak. That is why a technically correct
hand approach can still feel emotionally empty.

Author a sequence the audience can follow:
- One child notices the threat with eyes/head before the arm starts reaching.
- The chest and supporting foot change as the child checks or arrests momentum.
- The reaching hand opens and searches; the partner notices and receives it.
- Fingers close, the wrists settle and the connection changes both bodies.
- Their subsequent steps show a changed intention and relationship.

Use slight differences in response timing and action for a reason. A phase
offset or random noise does not by itself create individuality. For this story,
playful, cautious, urgent and exhausted movement each need different support,
spacing, posture and interaction. One loop with speed and palette changes cannot
express the whole progression.

4. THE DRAWING PACKAGE LIMITS THE ACTING

Severity: high.

The fixed profile faces, repeated hand shapes and rigid dress silhouette restrict
what the rigs can communicate. In v056 the added shoulder yoke changes a local
form, but the chest-to-pelvis relationship remains similar across steps and the
shape can read as a stiff bib. More deformation of that one part is unlikely to
produce the needed body performance.

Recommended method: retain the layered painted 2D production and use a hybrid of
rigged movement and authored replacement drawings. Use the rig for travel,
contact and broad posing; draw replacements where turning, compression, overlap
or anatomy changes the silhouette. Prioritize:
- Registered profile-to-look-back head poses, connected neck/hair/braid shapes,
  and eye/gaze or expression changes that remain readable at shot size.
- Relaxed, opening, reaching, receiving, clasping and supporting hands.
- Full torso/dress shapes for near/far loading, passing, release and protection.
- Connected shoulder/sleeve, elbow and wrist shapes at the actual posed joints.

Approve the rough silhouette and construction first, then restore the established
painting. Keep the current identities, proportions, costumes and palette; this
does not call for character redesign or volumetric 3D replacements.

The v057 three-method comparison is unfinished: A has a five-second movie; B
records eight pilot drawings out of 120 intended; C remains a reference decision.
It supplies no completed three-way result supporting a method choice. Unique
algorithmic drawing files are not evidence of good timing or strong posing.

5. CONTACT MUST COMMUNICATE WEIGHT AND CHANGE THE NEXT ACTION

Severity: high.

v059 has a useful attempt at stopping, turning and reaching, but its late contact
reads as reassurance. It does not yet show a secure grip taking the other's
weight, a convincing assisted recovery, and a completed resumed step. It also
uses an older character model, so direct promotion would regress consistency.

For support, stage the helper's brace and counterbalance, show where the load
passes through the hands, and keep the supported child's pelvis related to its
feet. Author the release/restart and outgoing step. A joined wrist distance is a
technical constraint; the body response makes it assistance.

6. FINAL COMPOSITION STILL AFFECTS HOW THE MOTION READS

Severity: high, with an important recent improvement.

Keep the v065f color-pipeline, clock, camera-route and depth-aware drawing reveal
work. The older blanket claim that the first-danger title hides the children is
superseded locally: the new reveal restores their visible drawings and respects
foreground-tree occlusion. The native START_HERE also records a changed downstream
camera route; later sections need to be rendered consistently when it is promoted.

However, the chronology still places feet and bodies against large black title
regions, sometimes removing the visible ground beneath a foot. This weakens
grounding even when the foot trajectory is correct. Check supporting ground,
hands and head separation through the actual moving title treatment. Reserve a
clear readable interval for the central decision/contact before the outgoing
tree occludes the girl. Keep the established typography treatment unless the
user chooses a change.

7. SECONDARY MOTION AND FINISHING ARE CURRENTLY LOWER-PRIORITY WORK

Boy-coat work is useful; girl dress, braid and ribbon still need follow-through.
Those should trail and settle from the accepted body action. Continuous sine-like
motion or generalized wobble would add activity without fixing intention.

Stage cloth compression/release, braid lag and hand settling after the primary
motion works. Rebuild dependent sleeve, leg and garment drawing caches after
changing the rig or its timing. Keep the source 30fps / production 24fps mapping
consistent; the existing 32-source-frame loop spans 25.6 production frames.

The painted environments, atmosphere and effects should support these actions.
Extra detail, more UHD exports and more local costume patches have low value
while the audience cannot read the children's performance.

THE NEXT BOUNDED PRODUCTION PASS

Use the existing 38–45s first-danger candidate: developed action at 39–44s, with
one second of neighboring motion on either side. This is the best first quality
benchmark because it tests walking, awareness, head direction, reaching, contact,
paired reaction and final composition in one short piece.

1. Freeze the selected art and component sources in a new assembly candidate.
   Keep v065f presentation/continuity fixes, the established painted world,
   current age-ten designs and the continuous color arc. Record each dependency
   and its status. Do not replace a character solely because its file is newer.

2. Create a performance pose board before dense keys or cloth work.
   Show about eight decisive whole-body moments: ordinary travel, notice,
   checked step, look/reach, receiving hand, settled contact, changed paired
   travel and outgoing step. Both characters must be posed as an interacting
   pair. Align reactions to the existing threat cue; the v060 source identifies
   flash peak G957. Verify its timing in the selected candidate before retiming.
   The music need not change for this acting pass.

3. Animate a rough version with deliberate spacing.
   Establish feet and mass together; add the head and hand drawings required
   by the acting. Allow a brief interruption or change of step if motivated.
   Maintain the neighboring route handles, or explicitly reconform and verify
   them if the new travel changes. Avoid merely layering angles over the old loop.

4. Review matched previous/new movies at normal speed, actual shot scale, both
   unmasked and through the final title. Include the full seven seconds and
   the outgoing step. Use the same camera, time and color conditions for A/B.
   Look for a visible decision and partner response, convincing support and
   a coherent silhouette. Close crops and numerical proofs are supporting views.

5. Add painting corrections, cloth/hair follow-through and restrained contacts
   only after primary movement passes the visual review. Preserve connected
   joints, drawing volume and the established design through every transition.

6. Integrate the accepted segment into one low-resolution full-film development
   review and update the coverage register. Then address play, threat/protection,
   escape, fatigue/recovery and ending in coherent sections. Each accepted section
   must advance the actual film. Produce UHD keys after acting/composition review;
   schedule the full UHD render after the integrated picture is ready.

VISUAL COMPLETION GATE FOR THE SEVEN-SECOND BENCHMARK

- The threat, notice, decision, reach and response are readable without explanation.
- The children have different, motivated timing and remain on the established model.
- The pelvis visibly accepts support; feet plant/release coherently with travel.
- The hand contact connects anatomically and influences the paired body motion.
- Head/neck, shoulders, wrists and costume contours stay connected and stable.
- The new intention continues through the first outgoing steps and both handles.
- The final title treatment preserves the action and enough ground to judge contact.
- A normal-speed review at final composition scale shows a clear improvement.

Do not count a save, a render, a decode, a new version number or smaller numerical
errors as passing this gate. No guarantee of an eventual numeric quality score is
implied. This project needs an animation judgment at each stage; the largest
remaining gains require stronger authored drawings, poses and performance timing.
