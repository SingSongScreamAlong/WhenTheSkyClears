The production problem
The existing world, a stable ordinary gait and a five-second reach integration are useful foundations. They do not yet deliver the trailer's performance. The emotional progression must be visible in the children's choices: play without vigilance, delayed recognition, a protective decision, shared fear, unequal exhaustion, accepting help, and finally continuing together. New ink-wash lighting and spatial depth should support those decisions. Surface treatment alone cannot supply them.

The current integration's steady v047 foundation is deliberately planar and restrained; the new v048 source candidate retains that drawing construction. Its own artistic assessment records fixed-profile heads, rigid painted hands, a charcoal/white-eye girl face inconsistent with the painted boy, rigid braid, deferred cloth, and no completed fatigue/helping/stop transitions. The v060 first-danger study changes the near arms and briefly pitches the heads while protecting the gait. It is an integration study at 39–44 seconds, not a complete fear reaction, turn, load-bearing clasp or full-film conform. The delivered v040 full trailer and v058 world still contain the inherited held pair outside such separate studies.

Film contract and timing
The original story source is source/handoff_extracted.txt, section 3. The later delivery contract in WHEN_THE_SKY_CLEARS_TRAILER_v040.txt establishes 3840x2160, 24fps, 2400 story frames plus 144 silent credit frames = 2544 frames / 106 seconds. Source-song cue timings have not been inferred here.

Time zero is F1. Section ranges below are inclusive, with the next section beginning on the next frame. Subdivisions are working acting allocations inside the existing story sections; they are not a new cut or a claim that those motions already exist. Maintain the continuous-looking lateral journey.

0–18s, F1–432: BEFORE
0–4, F1–96 — establish two independent, comfortable walkers. Use v047 as a starting pose/footfall reference. Their attention is on each other and the path, shoulders open, hands free. Different glance and arm phrasing should distinguish them; the existing +2-frame girl gait phase is already baked and should not be added again.
4–8, F97–192 — the boy chooses to skip ahead. Anticipation compresses over a planted foot, then a clear push, airborne accent and asymmetric landing. The girl notices and lengthens her next step rather than instantly copying his hop. Old PLAYFUL_SKIP supplies step-hop reference, not a current age-ten transition.
8–12, F193–288 — boy looks back and briefly walks backward to tease; girl catches up. This needs a true alternate facing/quarter-view head and torso, deliberate backwards foot placement and a passing-depth plan. Spinning the current profile card will collapse its drawing and expose incorrect far-side construction.
12–18, F289–432 — brief chase into the puddle leap. Make the playful hand invitation precede takeoff, not appear at landing. Separate their takeoff/landing timing; show ankle compression and body recovery, then resume travel without a root-speed kink. Missing assets: age-ten run/chase, jump anticipation, flight, landing and walk/run bridges.

18–35s, F433–840: SOMETHING CHANGES
18–24, F433–576 — continue play through the occluded environmental change. Let the children remain unaware for a few steps while the audience sees the empty village. Do not make every background change trigger a character flinch.
24–29, F577–696 — boy climbs/hops onto the low wall. Solve actual top contact, toe clearance and hip height against its geometry; allow a small, purposeful correction after landing. The girl chooses the safer route or follows with a different balance pose.
29–35, F697–840 — balanced travel on the wall. Arms have distinct counterweight functions, wrists/fingers relax and correct, chest counter-rotates against pelvis, gaze attends to the next placement. Use fixed foot contacts and visible top thickness. No completed balance sequence was found; this is custom performance and set-contact work.

35–50s, F841–1200: PLAY BECOMES CAUTION
35–39, F841–936 — playful jump lands among bricks; boy begins the next skip and aborts it. The arrested impulse needs a believable braking step, held balance and a head/eye decision. An unchanged cyclic gait cannot communicate that moment.
39–44, F937–1056 — first flash / girl notices, raises shoulders and offers her hand. Reuse the v060 staging, copied near-arm drawings, contact-space logic and tested 30-to-24fps mapping. Develop attention before the reach: eye direction, a readable head turn, thorax/shoulder rise, then hand offer; boy receives with his own delay. Existing small nods are only a placeholder. Replace the fixed painted grip with designed contact/curl shapes and a clear overlap silhouette.
44–50, F1057–1200 — offered touch becomes a sustained reassuring grip; both move lower and more purposefully. Author a connected walking/urgent-travel bridge. A held contact target must allow elbow breathing and torso compensation without turning the arms into rigid rods. The selected-arm v060 cache ends here; extend it deliberately, with new feet/root decisions if pace changes.

50–67s, F1201–1608: THE WORLD BEHIND THEM
50–54, F1201–1296 — light precedes impact. Their responses differ: boy instinctively ducks; girl recognizes, plants and commits to cover him. Shoulder/head timing should propagate through chest, pelvis and loaded feet. Preserve a moment of contact with the ground rather than lowering both rigs uniformly.
54–58, F1297–1392 — protective fold, hold a breath, then choose to run. This is the principal paired acting asset to build next after stable locomotion: clear arching girl silhouette over the boy, far-arm support/cover, visible boy response, uncluttered hand/head negative space, compressed legs and an asymmetric rise. v001 CAUTION_CROUCH offers only a simple lower/hold/rise prototype. The attempted camp_v004 has no delivered native.
58–62, F1393–1488 — urgent run through posts/canvas. Establish a real run with flight, different cadence/stride, head stabilization and purposeful forward inclination. Retain paired intention without identical leg phase or an impossible rigid clasp. No completed current-age10 run was found.
62–67, F1489–1608 — crater descent and scramble. Set geometry must supply actual contact normals, hand supports, usable steps and the crest. Downhill braking, a slip/catch and uphill push require distinct poses; a sheared flat walk is not sufficient. Girl first establishes support, then helps the boy negotiate the exit. Hands change from reassuring grip to a useful forearm/palm support where appropriate.

67–80s, F1609–1920: KEEP GOING
67–71, F1609–1704 — exhausted running loses amplitude and becomes uneven for a reason: shorter push, longer recovery, guarded chest, small gaze checks. Avoid restoring the old baseline's accidental pelvis lurch across every step. Fatigue is a shot-specific departure from stable gait.
71–76, F1705–1824 — boy catches a foot; girl brakes and turns. Author the caught toe, mass continuing, rescue foot plant and thigh/ground brace. Do not give both children the same stumble. The girl's body should anticipate the support before the boy starts rising.
76–80, F1825–1920 — help him up, move past the school window, then duck on the next distant flash without looking back. This repeats the danger response with learned anticipation. Old v007/v009 provide useful offered-touch/rise/restart timing, but their touch is explicitly light and their route nearly stops; they do not supply a load-bearing age-ten rescue. Design palm/forearm contacts, girl's planted support leg, boy's active push and the release/restart as one action. Keep the effort small within the landscape.

80–90s, F1921–2160: TITLE TERRAIN
80–85, F1921–2040 — wall-like forms resolve into title geometry. They step over a low base, briefly pass behind a projecting edge, then reappear before the contact is lost to the audience. One purposeful obstacle is more readable than a sequence of buoyant hops.
85–90, F2041–2160 — use the gap crossing to echo the earlier puddle: invitation becomes reassurance, landing now costs effort. Include toe clearance, grounded takeoff/landing, grip adjustment and a breath before continuing. This needs real letter-base contact geometry, not just the visible title-alpha mask.

90–96s, F2161–2304: SMALL AGAIN
Urgent travel decelerates to walking while still connected. Reuse v047 only after solving an authored speed/stride transition and paired hand height. Gaze moves to the route ahead; shoulders release slightly without triumph. Camera widening preserves the emotional scale and title reading. Keep the ten-second music fade on its existing 90–100s span.

96–100s, F2305–2400: QUIET
96–98, F2305–2352 — camera stops; children take their final few steps, then settle. Choose the last support foot, stop root travel on that contact, let pelvis/chest/head and cloth finish at different times. Do not freeze a moving cycle or slide it to a stop.
98–100, F2353–2400 — hold silhouettes and title together through the picture fade. A small shared hand adjustment or breath can complete the relationship; no large new gesture is needed.

100–106s, F2401–2544: SILENT CREDITS
Retain exact existing wording: WHEN THE SKY CLEARS. / The Animated Series. / 2027. / A Two Truck Productions project. No additional acting or repeated song fragment belongs here.

C. Shoulders, thorax and pelvis. Current rigs have exactly15 bones each: pelvis/chest/head plus near/far upper_arm, forearm, hand, thigh, shin and foot. Resolve quarter-view armholes, sleeve undersides, neck/collar and dress side surfaces before large bends.
D. Hands as performances. Build relaxed walk, open invitation, receptive palm, contact onset, thumb wrap, tightened grip, loose sustained grip, release, shield, thigh brace, ground palm and forearm-support shapes. Design both visible and occluded sides. Animate fingers as grouped masses with distinct thumb/index overlap; retain volume and knuckles through the curl. A closed shape or nearest-point distance alone does not establish a clasp.
E. Body mechanics library. Finish ordinary support/loading, walk-to-stop/idle, acceleration/deceleration, skip, backwards steps, child run, jump/landing, wall balance, duck/hunker/rise, downhill/uphill, toe catch/rescue step, help-up and exhausted restart. Make the two children make different choices; avoid applying one event identically with a small phase offset.
F. Secondary performance. After body timing is stable, braid/ribbon, coat hem and dress follow the measured acceleration/turn/impact, with delayed recovery and purposeful holds. Preserve silhouette and contact readability. Current v047 explicitly deferred this work. Cyclic noise is not a substitute for the change of direction or weight that causes motion.

Production sequence
First consolidate the latest stable ordinary-weight solution and the shared character acting model, keeping the current age-ten dimensions and liked paint. Complete the entire100-second action/root/camera layout in the new spatial environment using explicit key poses and the beat ranges above. This exposes missing travel bridges and contact geometry before surface work hides them.

Develop the protective fold at50–58s and the independent stumble/help-up at71–80s as the two central paired performances. They require the broadest new shoulder, quarter-view, hand and body construction and establish whether the system can act beyond a planar walk. Use their solved controls/drawings to finish the simpler first-danger reach, wall balance and title support. Build the playful opening and its puddle echo with the same mechanical and drawing consistency.

Then finish gaze/fingers, face/neck turns, cloth/braid and event-driven overlap across the conformed timeline. Review full normal-speed sequences and continuous joins for intention, weight, contact, silhouette and pauses. Frame sampling and numeric checks remain useful for catching defects, but do not certify emotional performance. Final ink-wash lighting, spatial atmosphere and edit should be judged with those performances in place.
