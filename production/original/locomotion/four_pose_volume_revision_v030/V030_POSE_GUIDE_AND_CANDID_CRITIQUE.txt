Loaded-down:

Up:

The support boot rolls to30degrees on the forefoot, lifting the ankle as the support leg extends.
Both guide leg segments remain260mm; the pose is not made by stretching a limb or lifting only the head.

Contour/arms:
Rounded quadratic knee caps and soft rear folds replace the point-like knee turns while retaining separate thigh/shin masses.
Shoulder attachments move with the restored torso envelope. Sleeves taper into the same small cuff design.
Front gestures are reduced toward hanging arms, and the rear contact hand is staged outside the coat hem.
These are four deliberately authored poses, not another angle/delay tuning pass on the old walk.

MY CANDID REVIEW OF THE ACTUAL RENDERS
Useful observed changes: the head is less dominant against the restored body; contact/down attitudes differ more;
the loaded pose moves pelvis/waist/ribcage together; knee turns are softer; passing boots separate more clearly;
up reads more like a forefoot-supported rise and the rear contact hand is visible.

Remaining weaknesses: the coat/ribcage still reads as broad graphic panels and can look armor-like.
The pelvis opening/value bridge is more connected, but its shape can still read as a patch rather than a convincing mass.
The passing/up hands sometimes merge with the hem/upper leg; arm staging is calmer but not finished natural acting.
The stronger loaded-down may feel too crouched in a future sequence. The expanded loaded-to-up range could also
become too bouncy if treated as a generic interpolated loop. The swing-knee/ankle projection and calf volume
need independent drawing review; a clear gap alone is not a natural gait.
The new torso/limb surfaces remain structural value block-in, visibly simpler than the painted head/collar/boots.
No dynamic weight, spacing, travel continuity, paired intention, support clasp or recovery restart has been demonstrated.
Please review these four constructions before deciding whether any inbetweens are justified.
