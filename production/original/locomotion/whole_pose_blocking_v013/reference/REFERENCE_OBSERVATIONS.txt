1. Eadweard Muybridge / University of Pennsylvania, Animal Locomotion,1887,
plate272, A woman tripping over a bed. Source: Wellcome Collection,27770i.
Primary catalogue https://wellcomecollection.org/works/tyvdbnzv
Downloaded reproduction, Wellcome Library, London / V0048664:
https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:A_woman_tripping_over_a_bed._Photogravure_after_Eadweard_Muy_Wellcome_V0048664.jpg
Local unmodified file: Muybridge_Plate272_Trip_Over_Bed_V0048664.jpg
OBSERVED: In the upper side-view row, the foot reaches the obstruction before
the torso pitches strongly forward; the two arms respond at different heights.
The body line changes substantially rather than just lowering upright hips.
Later images show a forward fall onto the bed with arms taking support.
LIMIT: Adult fall onto a soft bed, NOT a successful child's catch-and-recovery.
No claim that the later support strategy or exact timing matches our scene.
APPLIED AS AUTHOR CHOICE: Split boy's arms and push chest/head beyond his
current feet in the imbalance pose; make the urgent catching leg a separate
whole-pose breakdown. The successful catch is authored, not observed here.

2. Eadweard Muybridge / University of Pennsylvania, Animal Locomotion,1887,

Downloaded reproduction, Wellcome Library, London / V0048693:

OBSERVED: In upper side-view images3/4 the man remains compressed and folded.
By5 the legs/hips have begun to rise while the trunk is still pitched forward;
in6 the trunk follows more upright with the carried load near the body.
LIMIT: Adult lifting an external object, not a trip or a child using the thigh
to recover. No force magnitudes, child body mechanics or intervals measured.
APPLIED AS AUTHOR CHOICE: Low foot-gather and effort poses precede the boy's
push; hips rise before the chest fully follows. A near hand on the thigh is
an authored effort cue, not an action copied from this plate.
