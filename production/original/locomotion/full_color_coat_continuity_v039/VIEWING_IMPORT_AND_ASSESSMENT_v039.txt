WHAT CHANGED
Only the coat shape-key action and its compression-property action were replaced. The existing torso direction and hip loading drive continuous garment breakdowns at 0.25-source-frame intervals. Hat weights blend adjacent shapes linearly; Cycles modifiers repeat them over the same 31-frame period as the body.
Compression follows the original hip drop with a smoothstep transition. A restrained damped follower of the actual pelvis-to-neck direction gives the lower coat a small lag, smoothly limited to 1.6 degrees (actual maximum about 1.491 degrees). The upper garment is not independently reposed. The five-pose source construction, original collar/front painting, UVs, color palette and source alpha remain.
Original integer-frame coat vertical extents are retained through periodic interpolation of the original GP drawing extents. A small continuous hem response replaces static pose swaps. No independent fabric wobble clock, new lighting, outline, halo, bitmap repaint or design replacement was added.

CANDID ARTISTIC ASSESSMENT
The inspected native poses retain readable hands and a connected painted front without the earlier held coat-pose swaps. Hem and loading changes are deliberately small; the character identity and full-color separation survive at shot size. Native and decoded movie frames are reviewed for hand overlap, compression, hem continuity and material attachment. Continuous video-player playback observation is not claimed; the actual encoded movie is supplied for normal-speed human review.
The coat still reads fitted and flat in profile. Its existing conspicuous brush pattern supplies surface detail more strongly than three-dimensional fabric volume. This pass does not solve those design/construction limits. The passing feet remain crowded. Face/eye/profile planes, arm and hand contours, emotional intention, fatigue, stumbling, load-bearing clasp/support and a genuine recovery-to-walking restart remain unfinished. This does not approach the requested Arcane-level finish yet.
The next useful character decision is whether the moving color/coat direction holds up at normal speed. Further work should address specific visible construction/acting defects while preserving the movement direction the user preferred, rather than using more texture or lighting as a substitute for form.
